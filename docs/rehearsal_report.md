# Synthetic dress rehearsal of the evaluation protocol (S3.4, S4.2 to S4.6)

> **SYNTHETIC DATA.** Every number in this note comes from synthetic citation streams with planted cartels
> (`citecurv.data.synthetic`, `citecurv.eval.inject`). None of it is a measurement on real citations. The
> rehearsal tests the pipeline, the protocol and the component definitions against a known ground truth; it
> says nothing direct about how the method performs on the Retraction Watch labels.

All tables quoted here are generated by `python -m citecurv.eval.ablation --tables` from the run artifacts
(`results/rehearsal/tables.md`, CSV files next to it; `results/rehearsal/run_bundle.jsonl` regenerates them
byte for byte without the 1.3 GB of run folders). Diagnostics and compute figures come from
`results/rehearsal/rehearsal_summary.json`. Values are mean ± sample standard deviation over three seeds unless
a row says otherwise.

## 1. Short answer

- **Does the full model beat simple baselines?** No. On the three cartel levels the full model ranks planted
  cartel citations at or below chance (test AUROC 0.455, 0.419 and 0.534 for blatant, moderate and subtle),
  while a one-line control, the age of the cited paper at citation time, reaches AUROC 0.991 to 0.998 and the
  supervised XGBoost baseline 0.891 to 0.997. On the clique level the full model works (AUROC 0.858 ± 0.014,
  AP 0.155 ± 0.020) but stays below XGBoost (AP 0.829 ± 0.016) and the cited-age control (AP 0.637 ± 0.012).
- **Does removing curvature hurt?** No measurable effect. Under the protocol (weights calibrated on validation
  labels) every full minus no_curvature delta lies within seed noise or at chance level. With fixed equal weights,
  removing curvature *improves* the clique level (AUROC 0.651 ± 0.006 with curvature, 0.683 ± 0.022 without).
- **Why:** the planted cartels are separable by cited-paper age, which no component measures directly; s_temp
  ranks them *below* average because of a bias in its definition; cold start removes 34 % to 89 % of the
  positives from scoring; the validation calibration has too few positives to be stable; the Forman screen does
  not send cartel citations to ORC, and ORC does not separate them when computed for every positive.
- The pipeline mechanics pass: byte-identical rerun, cold-start consistency, D4 fairness, complete top-20
  breakdowns, frozen fitted objects under the timestamp shuffle. The shuffle gate itself fails on two of four
  levels, for a reason explained in section 6.

A negative answer is the expected outcome of a rehearsal whose job is to find problems before the real run;
section 8 lists what to change.

## 2. Setup

**Code path.** `scripts/rehearsal.py` drives everything through the package: `citecurv.pipeline.run_pipeline`
for each model cell, `citecurv.eval.ablation.run_matrix` for the matrix, the baseline modules for the baselines,
`citecurv.eval.metrics.evaluate` for every metric, and the `gate_*` functions of `citecurv.pipeline` for the
sanity checks. Environment: Python 3.12, GraphRicciCurvature 0.6.1, POT 0.9.6, networkit 11.2.1, scipy 1.13.1,
torch 2.15 (CPU), xgboost 3.4.1, scikit-learn 1.9.1; PyGOD 1.1.0 in its own environment. The plan pins
GraphRicciCurvature 0.5.3.2; the two versions gave identical unweighted directed ORC values in the S1.5 validation
(`docs/orc_version_comparison.md`).

**Stream (one per level and seed).** `generate_citation_stream(n_works=5000, n_fields=8, years=(2005, 2025),
refs_mean=25, emb_dim=64, seed=seed)`, about 121,000 to 126,000 events after injection. 5,000 works keep the
whole matrix, with real ORC, inside the 60 CPU-minute budget; smaller streams cannot host ten disjoint cartels
of up to 25 papers per window.

**Split.** Cutoff = the latest event time that leaves at least 20 % of events after it (2023-01-05 to
2023-02-23 depending on the seed); validation = the last 10 % of the pre-cutoff time span (`eval.split`).
Per realisation: 82,700 to 85,800 train, 13,500 to 14,400 val and 24,300 to 25,800 test events.

**Labels.** Cartels with disjoint member sets are planted in three windows: 10 in the train period (supervision
for XGBoost and one learned-head cell), 5 in the validation period (weight calibration), 10 in the test period
(evaluation, every member published after the cutoff). Levels: `blatant`, `moderate`, `subtle` use
`inject_cartel` with the `eval.inject.OBVIOUSNESS` presets (sizes 15-25 / 10-20 / 8-15 papers, internal density
0.6 / 0.35 / 0.2, reciprocity 0.9 / 0.6 / 0.3, windows 365 / 730 / 1460 days, recipient share 0.4, disjoint
author sets inside a cartel); `clique` uses `inject_clique` (15 papers per clique, no edge dropped). Positive =
injected event. Planted test events per realisation: 1,029 to 1,158 (blatant), 210 to 317 (moderate), 63 to 77
(subtle), 1,045 to 1,047 (clique).

**Seeds.** Seed s sets the stream, the cartels and the model seed. The seed spread is therefore the spread over
synthetic data realisations, which is larger than the model-seed spread the real experiment will report (on real
data only the model changes with the seed). Paired deltas compare cells on the same realisation.

**Model cells** (`eval.ablation.VARIANTS`): `full`, `no_curvature`, `forman_only`, `ollivier_only`,
`no_temporal`, `full_learned` (L13, logistic head fitted on validation labels, the same supervision as the
calibration), plus `full_learned_train` (the head fitted on train-period labels). s_embed runs in
`static_cosine` mode (the TGN is too slow on CPU for the matrix). Forman screen thresholds: the 1 % and 99 %
quantiles of training-row Forman values, strict tails. F <= 2 by construction and the 99 % training quantile
equals that maximum (hi = 2 in every run), so the strict upper tail (F > 2) never fires and the screen escalates
only the lower tail (F < -69 to -73 depending on the run), about 1.2 % of events.
ORC: radius 1, `OTDSinkhornMix`, alpha 0.5, edge cap 5,000 with pruning, no subsampling (about 1,320 to 1,670
ORC computations per escalation set, 0.030 s each on average; 0 to 5 subgraphs per run hit the cap with a lossy
cut). Components: k = 3, W = 90 days, min_cell = 30. Weights: AP-calibrated on the 0.1 simplex lattice over the
validation rows, or the fixed `scoring.weights` when validation has no scored positive.

**`no_temporal` (decision D11, PROVISIONAL).** No TGN memory (static embeddings for s_embed and s_curvdyn) and
s_temp switched off. This mirrors `no_curvature`, which removes every curvature-derived input. The flag
`pipeline.no_temporal_drop_s_temp = false` gives the note's encoder-only reading instead; with static embeddings
that reading leaves nothing to ablate and reproduces `full` exactly (checked in
`tests/test_pipeline_integration.py`).

**Sensitivity setting `equal_weights`.** The calibrated cells rerun with calibration off (weights 0.25 each), to
separate what the components carry from what the validation calibration picks.

**Baselines**, all on exactly the model's scored test events and through `evaluate()`: tier-1 rules
(self-citation, citing-venue Gini, burst z, reference overlap) and their rank average with train-row reference
distributions; tier-2 XGBoost (300 rounds, depth 4, eta 0.1, one thread) on tier-1 features, endpoint degrees and
embedding cosine, fitted on train-period labels; controls: cited-paper age at t (younger = more anomalous),
reference overlap, a seeded random score; PyGOD DOMINANT (100 epochs, hidden 64, undirected cutoff snapshot,
edge score = max of the endpoint scores) for the moderate level, seed 0 only (73 s of the budget for one run).

**Compute.** 36.4 CPU-minutes and 28.5 minutes wall clock for the whole rehearsal including the TGN run, under
`nice -n 10`; peak resident memory 1.46 GB in the driver and 1.54 GB in a child process.

- **Book test (iii).** The curvature-removal delta (full minus no_curvature) is within seed noise on every level
  and metric, with one formal exception: moderate AP, where the three per-seed deltas are +0.0005, +0.0006 and
  +0.0000. That passes the table's mechanical rule (same sign, larger than the seed spread), but both arms sit at
  the chance level (prevalence 0.0029), so it carries no information. With equal weights the clique delta is
  negative and consistent over seeds: ΔAP -0.009 ± 0.009, ΔAUROC -0.032 ± 0.023; curvature makes that ranking
  worse.
- **Identical cells.** `forman_only` equals `full` on every level: ORC adds nothing once the weights are
  calibrated, because the escalated set holds almost no positives (section 5). `no_temporal` equals `full` where
  the calibration had already given s_temp weight 0.
- **Learned head (L13).** Fitted on validation labels it beats the calibrated fixed weights on every level
  (ΔAUROC +0.104 ± 0.048 blatant, +0.248 ± 0.093 moderate, +0.054 ± 0.162 subtle, +0.022 ± 0.010 clique; ΔAP
  +0.052 ± 0.019 on cliques). It can give a component a negative weight, which the simplex cannot. Fitted on
  train-period labels it gains less (moderate ΔAUROC +0.175 ± 0.044) and loses on cliques (ΔAP -0.028 ± 0.004).

## 5. Why the components miss the cartels

**Which component carries signal** ("Which component carries the signal" table, standalone test AUROC of each z
on the scored test events): moderate s_embed 0.462 ± 0.016, s_geom 0.420 ± 0.024, s_temp 0.285 ± 0.069,
s_curvdyn 0.505 ± 0.047; clique s_embed 0.861 ± 0.010 (cliques join papers of different fields, so their cosine
is low), s_geom 0.452 ± 0.024, s_temp 0.304 ± 0.027, s_curvdyn 0.575 ± 0.043. No geometric component exceeds
0.58 on any level.

**Cited-paper age.** Both ends of a planted citation are published inside the cartel window, so the cited paper
is young: median age 448 days for scored moderate positives against 3,530 days for negatives (seed 0,
`rehearsal_summary.json`). The cited-age control and XGBoost (whose degree features are proxies of age) exploit
this; no model component measures it. This is a property of the injector, flagged by A4 (results/
synthetic_profile.json), not a finding about real cartels.

**s_temp is biased against young cited papers.** x = ln((n_recent + 1)/(n_before + 1)) + ln(T_before / W).
Under a constant citation rate r, x is close to 0 only when r W >> 1; for a rarely cited old paper the +1
pseudo-counts leave x near ln(T_before / W), which grows with the paper's age. Medians on the moderate scored test
events: negatives n_before 21, exposure 3,125 days, x 0.67; cartel positives n_before 3, exposure 244 days,
x 0.29. Ranked by s_temp, cartel citations fall below average (AUROC 0.29 to 0.41 alone).

**Cold start.** k = 3 excludes the first three citations of every paper. Cartel recipients are young, so their
first cartel citations are exactly the excluded events: of the test positives, 44 % (blatant), 74 % (moderate),
89 % (subtle) and 34 % (clique) are cold-start (scored positives 599.7 of 1,077, 64 of 249, 7.3 of 68.3, 693 of
1,046). Across all events the cold-start share is about 10 %.

**Calibration on few positives.** Scored validation positives per realisation: 263 to 342 (blatant, clique),
20 to 28 (moderate), 1 to 2 (subtle). The AP grid then puts all or most weight on one component, and which one
changes with the seed (moderate: s_geom 1.0, s_geom 1.0, mixed; blatant: s_temp 1.0, s_temp 0.8, s_curvdyn 1.0).

**Curvature selection and ORC.** Diagnostic on moderate seed 0 (labels choose the rows, so this is analysis, not
a model input): ORC computed for all 83 scored test positives and 1,000 sampled negatives gives AUROC 0.443 with
"higher kappa = more anomalous" (mean kappa -0.470 for positives, -0.444 for negatives). The screen escalated
1.2 % of the positives (1 of 83) and 1.9 % of the negatives; the matched random set of `ollivier_only` 1.2 %.
The screen's lower tail is citations of papers with long in-slice reference lists (F ~ 2 - out-degree of the
cited paper, A6/A2 finding), and the planted cartels are not among them.

## 6. Sanity gates (S3.4, S4.3)

| gate | result | detail |
|---|---|---|
| reproducibility (NFR4) | PASS | moderate seed 0 regenerated from scratch and rerun without the ORC memo: identical sha256 of scores.parquet (0506a863...), identical data hash |
| cold-start consistency | PASS 12/12 | flag equals a recount, totals NaN exactly on cold-start rows, z-statistics exclude them, every method counts the same test rows |
| D4 fairness | PASS 12/12 | largest (field of citing paper, year) share among the top 1 % test flags 0.084 to 0.167 (limit 0.5) |
| complete top-20 breakdowns (S3.4d) | PASS 12/12 | every top-20 test event has a finite z for all four components |
| timestamp shuffle | PASS 2/4 | fitted objects identical in all four; AUROC toward 0.5: moderate 0.408 -> 0.489, subtle 0.411 -> 0.455; not for blatant 0.397 -> 0.378, clique 0.869 -> 0.871 |

The shuffle permutes test-period timestamps and rescores with everything fitted on train and validation rows. That
those objects come out identical is the leak check, and it passes on every level. The ranking moves toward chance
only where the time-dependent component (s_temp) drives it. On the clique level the signal sits in the embedding
cosine, which does not depend on event order, so shuffling cannot remove it. On real data with TGN outputs the
shuffle needs a TGN inference on the shuffled stream, otherwise s_embed keeps the original order.

## 7. One real TGN run (separate, one seed)

Moderate cartels on a smaller stream (2,000 works, 4 fields, 49,118 events, 4 / 2 / 4 cartels), DyGLib TGN with
direction-aware messages, 2 epochs, learning rate 1e-3, one CPU thread: training 24.7 s, inference 5.5 s;
DyGLib's own link-prediction test AP 0.624; p_link above the negative's on 81.4 % of events. Full model with TGN
s_embed: test AUROC 0.671, AP 0.0066 (41 scored positives); the same stream with static cosine: AUROC 0.557, AP
0.0050. The TGN s_embed alone reaches AUROC 0.671 and s_curvdyn on TGN vectors 0.632 (0.500 on static vectors).
This shows that the TGN path runs end to end through the pipeline; with one seed and 41 positives it is not a
comparison.

## 8. What this does and does not tell us about the real-data experiment

It does tell us:

1. The pipeline, the ablation driver, the baselines and the table regeneration work end to end under the
   protocol, deterministically, within budget (36 CPU-minutes for 12 realisations of about 123,000 events, each
   scored by 12 model cells, 8 baselines and controls (9 with PyGOD) and the sanity runs).
2. Under fixed non-negative weights, a component whose orientation does not match the anomaly (s_temp here)
   pulls the total below chance, and validation calibration with tens of positives does not reliably remove it.
3. The cold-start rule removes most anomalous citations that involve young papers from evaluation.
4. The Forman screen, as defined, selects citations of papers with long reference lists; any anomaly type that
   does not involve such papers never reaches ORC.
5. Static detectors under the cutoff-snapshot protocol cannot score citations among post-cutoff papers.
6. A cited-age control must sit in every table: on this generator it beats every learned method.

It does not tell us whether the full model beats the baselines on Retraction Watch labels, whether curvature
helps on real reference lists, what SPECTER2 vectors and a trained TGN add at scale, or whether real
Referencing/Attributions cases look like these cartels. The synthetic vectors (d = 64) only encode field and
subtopic.

## 9. Recommendations

1. Report the cited-paper-age control and XGBoost in every real-data table, and call the model useful only if it
   beats both (protocol change, S4.4/S4.6).
2. Add an age-matched injection variant to `eval/inject.py` (members drawn so that cited ages match the natural
   distribution), so the synthetic track tests structure rather than age (owner of inject.py; also raised by A4).
3. Revise the s_temp statistic (docs/score_components.md D6, Maor): a surprise score whose expectation is 0 under a
   constant rate, e.g. a Poisson or Gamma-Poisson tail probability of n_recent given the paper's own past rate
   (close to tier-1 burst_z), then rerun this rehearsal.
4. Calibration: with fewer than about 100 scored validation positives, keep equal weights or calibrate on
   train + validation labels, and report `weights_source`; consider orienting each component by its sign on
   training labels, or the learned head as the main combiner (it won every level here).
5. Cold start (S2.4/L19): report the share of positives that are scored next to every metric, and consider
   scoring cold-start events with the components that need no history of the cited paper instead of excluding them.
6. Curvature screen: test an escalation rule tied to the ORC overlap signal (radius-1 ORC of an uncited citing
   paper is a closed form of the two reference lists, cheap enough to compute for every event), and rerun the
   curvature-removal delta with full ORC coverage before the real matrix.
7. Static baselines (S4.4): decide between the cutoff snapshot (current protocol, blind to post-cutoff papers)
   and a rolling snapshot per test month; record the choice as a human decision.
8. Shuffle test on real data: run the TGN inference on the shuffled stream in `40_tgn`, and read the gate as
   "time-dependent components degrade", not as a check on every component.
9. D11: confirm that `no_temporal` also removes s_temp (current default) or switch the flag.
10. Budget for the real run: ORC at radius 1 costs about 0.03 s per escalated event here; the ORC cache of
    `30_curvature` feeds every cell through `orc_memo_from_cache`, so each escalation set is computed once.
